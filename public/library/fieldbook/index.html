i.

This month's specimens

Each one drawn from the plant itself, leaf by leaf. Hover to lift it off the page.

"The garden does not care that I am watching. That is the whole pleasure of it."

The foxgloves came back. Three years after I gave up on them, after two summers of brown stalks and one of nothing at all, there they were by the shed: five spires, the tallest at my shoulder, the bells a deeper purple than the packet promised.

I have a theory, which is that plants remember. Not in the way we do, but in the way a path remembers feet. The soil by the shed was turned over for the first time in years last autumn, when the water pipe burst, and the seeds that had been lying there since the first sowing finally saw light.

So this week's lesson is patience, again. Mark the places where you planted things. Write it down. The book will remember when you forget.
